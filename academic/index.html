About Me

Johan Ajnabi

I am a PhD candidate in the Jamora laboratory at the BRIC-Institute for Stem Cell Science and Regenerative Medicine (inStem), where I study how tissue mechanics shape cell behavior during epidermal wound repair.

My work brings together mechanobiology, skin regeneration, and epigenetic regulation, using primary keratinocyte systems, fluorescence imaging, molecular biology, and transcriptomic approaches.

Research Overview

How cells interpret injury-associated mechanics

My work sits at the interface of mechanobiology, cell biology, and epigenetics, with a focus on how wounded skin converts physical cues into durable changes in cellular behavior.

At the Jamora laboratory, I study how actin-dependent mechanics, signaling pathways, and chromatin regulators coordinate epidermal cell-state transitions during tissue repair. I am especially interested in questions where physical microenvironments meet nuclear decision-making.

Current Questions

  • How wound-associated mechanics reposition chromatin regulators such as DNMT3A.
  • How actin remodeling and ERK signaling shape epidermal cell-state transitions.
  • How physical cues intersect with regeneration, stemness, and disease in skin.
